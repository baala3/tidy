// 01 · What it clears

The stuff that piles up while you're not looking.

Tidy only lists folders it recognizes, and tells you why each one is safe. Anything it doesn't recognize never shows up.

01 / Caches

App caches

Temp files, crash dumps, error reports, shader caches, and browser caches for every profile. Passwords, history and sign-ins are never touched. An open browser is skipped until you close it.

Chrome · Edge · Brave · Firefox · Vivaldi · Opera
02 / Dev

Dev junk

Package manager and editor caches, plus build output in projects you haven't touched in a while. Projects you're still working on are skipped.

npm · NuGet · Cargo · node_modules · .venv
03 / Apps

Apps

The games, promos and trials that came with Windows or your PC, each with a note on what it is, and apps you haven't opened in 90 days. Runtimes, codecs and drivers are never offered.

Preinstalled extras · unused for 90 days
04 / Large files

Large files

Old downloads, installers, videos, disk images and AI models in your own folders, with search and filters. Nothing is selected for you, and OneDrive-only files are left alone.

// 03 · How it works

Most cleaners try to find more. Tidy tries to be sure.

Every line is public on GitHub. The safety page lists everything Tidy may clear and everything it refuses, generated from the app's own rules.

  1. It only lists what it recognizes

    Caches come from a fixed allowlist. Anything behind a link or junction is skipped.

  2. You see why before you choose

    Each item is marked Safe to Clean or Check First, with a plain-language note. Large files and apps are never selected for you.

  3. It checks again at the last moment

    Every path is re-checked against the safety rules right before it moves, not just when it was scanned.

  4. The Recycle Bin, not the void

    Cleared files go to the Recycle Bin. If Windows would delete something for good, Tidy leaves it alone instead.

// 04 · Questions

Before you clean.

Is Tidy safe to use?

Caches are cleared only from a fixed allowlist, and anything Tidy doesn't recognize never shows up. Each path is checked again at the moment it's moved. Large files and apps are never selected for you, and nothing goes until you confirm. The safety page lists everything Tidy may clear and everything it refuses, generated from the app's own rules.

Will it delete anything permanently?

Files and folders go to the Recycle Bin, and emptying it is your call. If the Recycle Bin is turned off for a drive, or an item is too big for it, Tidy skips that item rather than delete it for good. Uninstalled apps are the exception: they can't go to the Recycle Bin. Store apps can be reinstalled from the Microsoft Store, and Tidy says so before you confirm.

What are "preinstalled extras"?

Apps that came with Windows or your PC rather than ones you chose: games like Solitaire and Candy Crush, News, Tips, Get Help, streaming promos, and maker add-ons from HP, Lenovo, Dell, ASUS and others. Each is marked Safe to Remove or Check First with a note on what it does. The Store itself, Windows Security, Xbox sign-in parts, codecs and drivers are never offered.

How does it know which apps I don't use?

Windows keeps a record of apps you open from Start, the taskbar and File Explorer. Tidy reads it on your PC; nothing leaves your machine. Apps you only start another way, like from a terminal, show "No launch recorded" instead of a date. If you turned app launch tracking off in Windows privacy settings, Tidy says so and doesn't guess.

Why does Windows say "Windows protected your PC"?

Tidy isn't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen warns about the downloaded installer. Choose More info, then Run anyway. The PowerShell install command doesn't trigger the warning, and it checks the installer against the published SHA-256 before running it.

Does it need admin rights?

No. Tidy cleans your own folders and installs for your account only. Some desktop apps ask for permission when their own uninstaller runs; that request comes from the app, not Tidy.

Why didn't my free space go up right away?

Files in the Recycle Bin still take space. The space comes back when you empty it. Tidy shows how much is sitting there, so the number isn't a surprise.

Does Tidy collect any data?

No. No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no account. Tidy doesn't connect to the internet at all.

Is it really free?

Yes. No license, no trial, no paid tier, nothing bundled in. It's MIT licensed, and the full source is on GitHub.

How do I uninstall it?

Uninstall Tidy from Settings > Apps like any other app. If you used the portable zip, close Tidy and delete its folder.
